Text search assumes the useful evidence can become words first. That breaks when someone remembers a slide layout, chart shape, screenshot, video moment, or PDF figure.

Multimodal embeddings let those artifacts participate in retrieval. The main design choice is the retrieval unit:

- PDF section, page, or figure crop
- sampled video window or scene
- full image or object crop
- table snippet rather than the entire document

Segmentation decides whether the index can find the evidence at all.

Scores need calibration too. Text-to-text, image-to-text, and video-to-text similarities may have different distributions. A score of 0.78 is not automatically comparable across lanes.

A mixed retrieval path can stay explicit:

~~~text
query -> encode
      -> retrieve text
      -> retrieve image
      -> retrieve video
      -> apply permission filters
      -> rerank mixed candidates
~~~

Reranking is where the product can distinguish the best screenshot match from the best page match instead of pretending all vectors share one scale.

Hard negatives make the index useful: a red error banner versus a red success banner, a slide with the right title but wrong chart, two similar video clips with different actions, or a PDF page where the phrase appears only in a footer.

Multimodal embeddings match how people remember work. They do not remove the usual retrieval responsibilities: choose the segment, preserve metadata, filter permissions early, calibrate scores, and test the close failures. The map gets wider only when the product keeps it readable.
