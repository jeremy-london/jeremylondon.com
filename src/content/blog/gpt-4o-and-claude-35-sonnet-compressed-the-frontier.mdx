GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet made frontier capability feel closer to ordinary product work.

The important comparison was not which model won. Latency, price, visual input, and coding behavior all became routing choices. "Best model" stopped being a sufficient product description.

The routes looked more useful:

| route | needs | reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| support triage | speed and text understanding | answer quickly |
| screenshot analysis | vision and explanation | inspect UI states |
| coding help | reasoning and patch discipline | keep diffs usable |
| long-form drafting | economical tokens | avoid wasting budget |

A model does not need to be globally strongest to be the right route. It needs to be fast enough, affordable enough, and reliable on the task slice that matters.

That changes evals too. Measure patch quality, screenshot reasoning, tool stability, latency, cost, and recoverability. A model that scores lower on a broad benchmark may still be the better product route if it handles the important slice and fails in a way the application can recover from.

The frontier compressed because the middle became good enough to ship. That raised the standard around the model: route by job, verify the output, and optimize the workflow rather than the trophy.
