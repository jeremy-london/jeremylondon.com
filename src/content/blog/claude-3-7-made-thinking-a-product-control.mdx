Claude 3.7 Sonnet made reasoning feel less like a hidden model trait and more like a product control.

The important change was not only that the model could be more capable. Anthropic exposed a hybrid path: answer quickly or spend a defined amount of time on extended thinking. Once that choice is visible, the product has to own it.

Extra reasoning should have a reason. A difficult debugging task, a multi-file change, or a high-consequence comparison may justify the delay. A sentence rewrite or known-field extraction usually does not.

A basic route can be enough to start:

~~~text
fast:
  formatting, extraction, low-risk rewrites, short summaries

think:
  debugging, planning, multi-file changes, policy comparisons, hard tradeoffs

ask:
  expensive batches or latency above the user's expectation
~~~

The labels are less important than making the decision measurable.

When a model visibly takes longer, users judge more than the answer. They also judge whether the time was well spent. A slow response is not evidence of a correct response, and a long internal process can turn a simple task into a worse one.

Thinking budgets belong in routing metadata rather than vague prompt language:

~~~json
{
  "task": "debug failing integration test",
  "risk": "medium",
  "latencyBudgetMs": 20000,
  "requiresPlan": true
}
~~~

With structured routing, the team can ask useful questions. Did extended thinking improve this task family? Did it reduce retries? Did it help plans but not execution? Did it add cost to work that was already acceptable on the fast path?

The fast path still needs discipline. Low latency does not mean weak context, missing schema validation, or casual scope handling. A quick extraction can need strict validation. A slow architecture decision can still need human review.

Coding agents make the distinction especially clear. An agent might inspect a failure quickly, switch to deeper reasoning when several modules disagree, return to a fast edit, and verify the result with a focused test:

~~~text
inspect failure: fast
compare recent diff: fast
plan cross-package repair: extended thinking
apply focused edit: fast
verify regression case: fast
~~~

That is more useful than a global "think harder" switch because it records where the extra time went.

Reasoning controls have failure modes too. The model can overwork an easy task, produce a plan larger than the change, or use additional thought to defend a bad assumption. Longer thinking is not a substitute for tests, retrieval quality, policy checks, or approval.

Claude 3.7 made the tradeoff visible. The product now has to decide when carefulness earns its latency, how much budget it gets, and what evidence proves the extra reasoning helped.
